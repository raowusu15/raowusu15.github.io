Robert Afiakwa Owusu

Biomedical engineer working in computational bioengineering, with a core focus on computer-aided drug discovery and medical imaging.

I am a research assistant on the Global Health and Infectious Diseases AI team at the Kumasi Centre for Collaborative Research (KCCR), Kumasi, Ghana. I work on understanding biological systems computationally — building the models, pipelines and data infrastructure that turn biomedical data into something a researcher or clinician can act on.

Two problems occupy most of my time. The first is computer-aided drug discovery: I am building the West African Traditional Medicine Systems Pharmacology platform, which brings the pharmacological knowledge held in West African medicinal plants into a computational framework, and I run structure-based screening and cardiotoxicity prediction workflows alongside it. The second is medical imaging: deep learning for segmentation, and the question of whether a model that scores well on a benchmark still holds up when the image quality degrades the way it does in a real clinic.

I graduated first class from KNUST in biomedical engineering as departmental valedictorian. Before the computational work I built hardware — a low-cost wireless digital stethoscope for use in Ghanaian clinics — and spent two internships maintaining diagnostic equipment in Ghanaian hospitals. That still shapes how I judge a method: it has to survive the conditions it was built for.

I teach as well. I am a teaching assistant at KNUST for biosignals analysis, digital signal processing and biomedical optics, and I tutor online through my YouTube channel and with Academic Special Assistance Portal (ASAP Studies and Study Clinic), a US-based tutoring company.
